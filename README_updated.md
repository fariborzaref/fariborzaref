## Research Perspective

My research examines how social structures and institutions shape inequality, mobility, and social change, and how sociological evidence can explain those processes across countries and historical periods.

I work across **comparative sociology, social stratification, and research methodology**, combining theoretical analysis with quantitative, qualitative, and computational approaches. A central concern runs through this work: **how do institutional arrangements transform differences in resources and opportunities into durable social advantages or disadvantages?**

> The purpose of research is not simply to describe differences, but to identify the mechanisms that produce and sustain them.

## Research Areas

| Area | Guiding concern |
|:--|:--|
| **Inequality & Stratification** | How class, economic institutions, and social policies reproduce advantage and disadvantage |
| **Comparative & Global Sociology** | How national institutions shape different patterns of inequality and mobility |
| **Institutions & Social Change** | How crises, labor markets, technological change, and migration reshape opportunities |
| **Methods & Evidence** | How research design, measurement, and analytic choices support credible explanations |

My research also addresses welfare regimes, education, health disparities, demographic transitions, and the relationship between computational tools and sociological explanation.

## Methodological Approach

I treat methodology as part of explanation, not a separate technical exercise. My work draws on:

- **Quantitative research:** multilevel and longitudinal models, regression and generalized linear models, structural equation modeling, and generalized estimating equations.
- **Qualitative research:** interviews, thematic and discourse analysis, institutional analysis, ethnographic approaches, and interpretive research.
- **Computational research:** text analysis, social network analysis, simulation, and reproducible analytical workflows.
- **Mixed methods:** integrating forms of evidence around a coherent research question and research design.

## Teaching

My teaching emphasizes the movement from **research questions to evidence, from evidence to analysis, and from analysis to sociological explanation**. I aim to help students distinguish plausible answers from defensible conclusions.

Teaching areas include research methods, social statistics, social theory, social stratification and inequality, social problems, social psychology, race and ethnic relations, and educational research.

## Open Science

The repositories linked here are intended to support transparent and reusable research practices. Their organizing principle is simple: a reader should be able to understand not only **what** an analysis concludes, but **how** those conclusions were reached.
