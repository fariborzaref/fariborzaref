01 / PERSPECTIVE

A sociological lens on a changing world.

Social inequalities do not emerge in isolation. They are produced, maintained, and sometimes transformed through the institutions, relationships, and historical conditions that organize everyday life.

My research connects sociological theory with comparative inquiry and quantitative, qualitative, and computational approaches. Across substantive topics, I return to the same challenge: how to move from observing patterns to explaining the social processes that generate them.

“Good social science makes complex realities more visible, more explainable, and more accountable.”

06 / TEACHING

Teaching students to ask better questions.

Education in sociology begins with curiosity, but it becomes meaningful through careful reasoning, evidence, and the willingness to question assumptions.

My teaching emphasizes the full research process: defining a question, evaluating evidence, selecting methods, interpreting results, and building an explanation that can withstand scrutiny.
